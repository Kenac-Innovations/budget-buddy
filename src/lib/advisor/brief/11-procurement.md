# Public procurement (PRAZ) and imported hardware lead times

Status: DRAFT, awaiting Kenac review
Review date: 2026-10-14 (draft preparation; no Kenac sign-off)
Next review: 2027-01-14, or sooner if source guidance changes

## What it is
Public-entity procurement planning needs an approved process aligned to the financial year. PRAZ publishes procurement plan templates, guidance and statutory instruments. Imported hardware delivery must be confirmed for the actual specification.

## Who it fits in Zimbabwe
Planning judgement: ask whether the organisation is a public entity, then investigate the approval and procurement timetable with its procurement team. Keep usable assets where replacement is not yet justified.

## When it is hype or premature, and prerequisites
Premature: committing to an installation month before approvals or treating an indicative shipping promise as a guarantee. Prerequisites: current PRAZ guidance, internal approvals, a neutral specification and a dated delivery commitment.

## Common failures
Common failures to check: omitted evaluation time, commissioning after branch opening, substitution without fit assessment or missing logistics and disposal scope. Never invent a tender threshold, mandatory procedure or import lead time.

## Questions to ask
What approvals must precede procurement? Has the delivery and commissioning sequence been confirmed?

## Sources with dates
[PRAZ downloads](https://www.praz.org.zw/downloads/), accessed 2026-10-14; includes annual procurement plan templates and current instruments. Import timing remains a question for dated evidence, not a local-market claim.

Local fit, sequencing and failure checks are draft planning judgements for Kenac to review. They are conditional, not assertions of local prevalence or guaranteed outcomes.
